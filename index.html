What is steganography?

Steganography is the practice of hiding information inside something ordinary — an image, an audio clip, a document — so that its very existence goes unnoticed. Where encryption makes a message unreadable, steganography makes it invisible: to anyone looking, the carrier file is just a normal photo or sound. The two are often combined, so a hidden payload is both concealed and, if found, still encrypted.

StegApp works on the steghide model: a secret file is woven into the least significant details of a carrier (a JPEG, BMP, WAV, or AU file) using a passphrase. The result looks and plays back identically to the original, but the passphrase can later pull the payload back out.

Where it's used

Security research & CTFs

Capture-the-flag challenges routinely hide keys inside media files; detecting and cracking them is a core skill.

Digital watermarking

Studios and photographers embed invisible ownership marks to trace leaks and prove provenance.

Private communication

Journalists and activists under surveillance can pass messages that don't even look like messages.

Malware analysis

Attackers smuggle payloads and commands inside images; defenders learn to spot the same techniques.

A quick workflow

  1. Embed a secret into a carrier with a passphrase.
  2. Extract it back out when you know the passphrase.
  3. Detect whether an unknown file is hiding anything at all.
  4. Crack a suspected carrier against a wordlist to recover the passphrase.
